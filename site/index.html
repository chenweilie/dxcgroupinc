Fit

Where AI helps, and where it does not

We say so early when a process is not worth automating. It saves both of us time.

Where it helps

  • High-volume, repetitive work
  • Inputs that vary in format: PDFs, email, spreadsheets
  • Clear rules for what “correct” looks like
  • Mistakes that a reviewer can catch

Where it does not

  • Rare, one-off tasks
  • Decisions with no written criteria
  • Irreversible actions with no review step
  • Data that cannot be shared under any terms

Principles

How we approach the work

01

Measure first

We baseline the current process before changing it, so improvement is something you can check.

02

Review built in

Exceptions and sensitive actions go to a person, with the reason attached.

03

Reversible by design

Every workflow has logging and a clear way to switch it off.

04

Documented

What was built, what data it touches and how to run it, written down.

Approach

Small, measured, reversible

  1. Assess

    A short, fixed-scope review of one process: steps, volumes, error cases and who touches what.

  2. Prototype

    A working version on real samples, so you judge results rather than slides.

  3. Deploy

    Rolled out with human review on, logging on and a clear way to turn it off.

  4. Improve

    We review the exceptions together each month and tighten the rules.

FAQ

Common questions

Do you work with clients remotely?

Yes. All engagements are delivered remotely through video calls, shared documents and the tools your team already uses, such as your ticketing system, spreadsheets or ERP.

Which processes are a good fit for AI automation?

High-volume, rules-heavy work with semi-structured inputs such as invoices, orders, support tickets and contracts, where a person can review the exceptions. Rare one-off tasks and decisions without written criteria are usually not worth automating.

How do you handle sensitive data?

We ask for the minimum access an engagement needs, prefer to work inside accounts and systems you control, and agree data handling, retention and deletion in writing before work starts.

How does pricing work?

We start with a fixed-scope assessment. Build and support work is then quoted per project or as a monthly retainer. You see the scope and price before anything starts.

Which AI models do you use?

We choose by task, cost and your data requirements, and we tell you which provider is involved and what data would be sent to it. Where rules are enough, we use rules instead of a model.

Will this replace our staff?

The goal is to remove repetitive steps so people spend their time on exceptions and judgement. Staffing decisions are yours, and we do not promise headcount changes.

Have a process in mind?

Tell us about it. A few lines are enough, and we reply within two business days.
