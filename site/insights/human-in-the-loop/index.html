Designing human review into AI workflows

How to place people in an AI-assisted workflow so errors are caught early and trust builds over time.

DXC Group Inc. · 2026-10-30

An AI-assisted workflow without a review step is a risk. One with a badly placed review step is a bottleneck. The goal is to put people where their judgement matters and keep them away from routine work.

Route by confidence and by consequence

Send an item to a person when the system is unsure, or when the action is costly or hard to undo, even if the system is sure. Everything else can go through.

Show the reason

A reviewer who sees only “flagged” has to redo the work. A reviewer who sees “amount differs from the purchase order by 4%” can decide in seconds.

Keep an exception queue, not a free-for-all

One queue with owners and a time target keeps exceptions from piling up. Track what lands there, because that list tells you which rules to improve next.

Log every decision

Record what the system did, what the reviewer decided and when. This supports audits, debugging and the conversation about whether to loosen or tighten review.

Plan the off switch

Decide in advance how to pause the workflow and how to revert its changes. A workflow you can stop is a workflow people are willing to start.

Loosen review gradually

Start with review on everything, then relax it for categories where results have been consistently correct. Let evidence, not enthusiasm, drive that decision.
